# Synthetic Media Policy

**Organization:** the Firm, a fictional, concretely characterized 250-person regional accounting and advisory firm (see context below)
**Applies to:** all employees, contractors and interns
**Policy owner:** Head of People
**Version:** 1.0 (adoption draft)
**Review:** every 6 months, after any incident, and it lapses after 12 months unless renewed (Section 11)

## Context this policy is written for

The Firm has about 250 staff across three offices, with roughly 40% working remotely or in the field and about 90 hourly or administrative employees who receive most information through the intranet, email and team chat. Internal Communications is four people (a Lead, two writers, one video producer); People (HR) is six. Staff work in English and Spanish. Internal video is the fastest-growing way the Firm explains things: policy changes, onboarding, IT how-tos, benefits enrolment. Synthetic presenters and cloned voices are tempting because they are fast, cheap, and multilingual. They are dangerous here because employees trust the intranet by default, leaders' voices and faces carry authority, Finance moves money on instruction, and the Firm's staff and leaders appear in recorded video that could be copied.

---

## 0. The short version

Synthetic media means AI-generated voices, faces or bodies that appear to speak or act. At the Firm we use it only for routine internal information, delivered by an approved synthetic presenter that is visibly labeled and resembles no real person. We never use it to speak as a real person without their written consent, never to deliver news that affects someone's job, pay, health or safety, and never for anything that leaves the Firm. Every piece has a named human owner, an in-frame label, and an entry in the register. If you are unsure, do not make it; ask the Steward.

---

## 1. Definitions

- **Synthetic media:** audio, video or images in which an AI system generates a voice, face or body that appears to speak or act.
- **Approved synthetic presenter:** a fully generated voice or face, from a vendor the Steward has approved, that does not resemble any identifiable real person. **Resemblance test:** if a colleague could plausibly say "that sounds like (or looks like) X," treat it as X's likeness, not a synthetic presenter.
- **Likeness:** a real person's voice, face, body, or distinctive mannerisms.
- **Responsible Human:** the employee who owns the content and is accountable for its accuracy.
- **Register:** the Firm's log of all synthetic (and authentic executive) media (Section 7).

## 2. Roles

- **Responsible Human:** requests the piece; their name appears on it.
- **Synthetic Media Steward (the Internal Communications Lead):** keeps the register, does first review, may refuse.
- **Second Reviewer:** the Head of People for HR content; the General Counsel/Privacy Officer if any real person's likeness, personal data or legal topic is involved; otherwise the Operations Director. Never someone in the requester's reporting line.
- **Executive Sponsor (COO):** hears escalations on tier classification. Cannot waive Section 4.

---

## 3. Permitted uses

**Tier A: Steward sign-off only.** Approved synthetic presenter only.
- **A1.** Narrating already-approved written internal content that has a source document: policy explainers for existing policy, onboarding modules, IT how-tos, benefits-enrolment walkthroughs.
- **A2.** Read-aloud audio versions of written internal documents, for accessibility.
- **A3.** Translated captions or audio of Tier A content, after a fluent human has reviewed the translation.
- **A4.** Training role-plays where the characters are clearly fictional and labeled.

**Tier B: Steward and Second Reviewer.**
- **B1.** A real employee's own voice or likeness, with their written consent (Section 5), for a defined purpose, such as dubbing their own recorded training into Spanish.
- **B2.** Content describing a proposed or decided Firm-wide change (for example, an analysis of whether to pilot a four-day week). The decision-maker must confirm in writing that the script is accurate, and caveats follow rule 6.8.
- **B3.** Piloting a new tool or vendor.

**Anything not listed above is not permitted** until the Steward classifies it. The default answer is no.

---

## 4. Prohibited uses (no exceptions; no one can waive these)

| # | Prohibited | Why |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | Cloning or imitating the voice or face of any real person (employee, former employee, executive, client, public figure, deceased person) without valid written consent under Section 5. "Just a test" and "parody" are not exceptions; tests use approved presenters only. | Nobody's voice should be usable without their say. |
| P2 | Putting words in a real person's mouth that they have not read and approved in writing, even with likeness consent. | Consent to a voice is not consent to every sentence. |
| P3 | Delivering, by synthetic voice or face, any communication that materially affects a person's employment, pay, bonus, benefits, discipline, health, safety or legal position. This includes hiring, redundancy, performance, disciplinary, medical and emergency communications. | Consequential news needs an accountable human who can be looked at and questioned. |
| P4 | Any message that instructs or authorizes action involving money, credentials, system access or sensitive data (payments, vendor bank-detail changes, password resets), regardless of the voice used. | This format is the template for impersonation fraud. |
| P5 | Any external use: clients, prospects, press, investors, regulators, recruiting, social media, the public website. (Internal-only in v1.) | The Firm cannot control labeling or context outside its walls. |
| P6 | Synthetic testimonials, invented quotes, or personas presented as real staff, clients or experts. | Fabricated voices of people who do not exist are still deception. |
| P7 | Use of a former employee's likeness. Consent ends automatically on separation and can only be renewed in writing after the person has left. | Marta's-voice problem: authorization does not outlive the relationship. |
| P8 | Removing, cropping, shortening or obscuring the label or the vendor watermark; exporting unlabeled excerpts. | Labels are the minimum safeguard. |
| P9 | Using unapproved tools or personal accounts; uploading Firm confidential information, personal data or third-party voice samples to any unapproved tool. | Shadow tooling leaks data and skips review. |
| P10 | Using synthetic media to evaluate or monitor employees, as HR investigation material, or to create synthetic "evidence" of anything. | Synthetic content is never evidence. |
| P11 | Producing individually tailored versions of a message for different employees or groups (translations under A3 excepted). | Personalized synthetic messaging is the scale-and-deception risk. |

## 5. Consent workflow

Applies to Tier B1 and any use involving a real person's likeness.

1. **Who asks.** The Responsible Human submits the request to the Steward; **People (HR), not the requester and not the person's manager, asks for consent.**
2. **The consent form** states: which likeness (voice, face, or both); the specific project and content; the channels; maximum duration; whether translations are allowed; the tool and vendor; how source samples are stored; and the person's right to review the final output.
3. **Voluntary.** Refusal has no consequence, and the form says so. Consent is never part of an employment contract or onboarding pack, and the person gets at least **5 business days** to decide.
4. **Documentation.** HR files the form and gives the person a copy. The register entry references the consent ID.
5. **Validity.** Maximum **12 months**, one purpose. A new purpose needs new consent. Consent ends automatically on separation (P7).
6. **Review right.** The person sees and approves the finished piece (and any translation) before publication.
7. **Revocation.** The person can revoke at any time by email to HR. Within **5 business days** the Firm stops new generation, unpublishes from Firm channels, and updates the register. Within **30 days** it deletes the person's voice or face samples and any model built from them, and obtains the vendor's deletion confirmation where the vendor offers one. The form tells the person up front that content already viewed cannot be recalled.
8. **No proxies.** Nobody can consent on behalf of someone else, including for deceased or departed people.
9. **Stock presenters.** The Steward keeps each vendor's licence and sourcing terms on file and rechecks annually. If a vendor cannot say whether its stock voices or faces are generated or consented, the Firm does not approve that vendor.

## 6. Disclosure standards

6.1 **Spoken:** the piece opens, within the first 5 seconds, with: "This video uses an AI-generated presenter." (In the language of the content.)
6.2 **On screen:** a label in the **lower third of the frame, not a corner**, for the full duration: "AI-generated presenter. Content owner: [name, department]."
6.3 **Audio-only:** spoken disclosure at the start and end, and in the title and description.
6.4 **Intranet post:** a text label above the player, a transcript, and a link to the register entry.
6.5 **Filename:** ends `_SYNTHETIC`.
6.6 **Vendor watermarks:** do not remove them, and do not rely on them; the Firm's own label is always burned in as well.
6.7 **Excerpts:** any exported segment shorter than 20 seconds requires Steward approval, and every approved segment carries the in-frame label. Audio-only clips under 30 seconds are not exported. Share links to the full video, not clips. Report any unlabeled excerpt you see.
6.8 **Caveat-proximity rule:** any important caveat must sit in the same segment as the claim it qualifies, so that no 20-second excerpt can drop it.
6.9 **Translations:** labels are translated into the language of the content.

---

## 7. Provenance and the register

7.1 **The register** (restricted-access spreadsheet, with a read-only list on the intranet) records: unique ID; title; date; Responsible Human; Steward; Second Reviewer; tool and version; voice or presenter; settings; script version; consent IDs; channels; SHA-256 hash of the published file; status.
7.2 **Content credentials:** where the tool supports them, export with them on; where it does not, note that in the register. **Do not rely on them**: they are often stripped when files are re-encoded or screen-recorded.
7.3 **Retention:** masters, scripts and hashes are kept for 3 years.
7.4 **Authenticity rule for all staff:** a synthetic video or audio message is official only if it is listed in the intranet register. Anything else, including any message in a leader's voice or face, is **unverified**.
7.5 **Callback rule:** nobody acts on an instruction involving money, credentials, or access received only by voice or video. Verify by calling back on a number you already have, or in person.
7.6 **Authentic recordings:** genuine recordings of executives for all-staff communications are also entered in the register, marked "recorded, not synthetic," so the register can confirm what is real as well as flag what is synthetic.

---

## 8. Review and approval

**Process.**
1. **Request form** (Responsible Human): purpose, audience, script and source documents, tool, presenter, whether a real person is involved, channels.
2. **Steward triage** (within 1 business day): Tier A, Tier B, or prohibited.
3. **Review** (up to 3 business days).
4. **Pre-publication check** by the Steward: label present in frame, hash logged, register complete.
5. **Post-publication check** at 7 days: no unlabeled excerpts in circulation.

**The standard.** Approve only if every answer is yes:
1. Is every factual claim traceable to a source document the reviewer has actually opened?
2. Would a reasonable viewer know it is synthetic and who stands behind it?
3. If any real person's likeness is involved, is consent complete and current?
4. If a viewer saw only any 20-second excerpt, would they be misled?
5. Would we be comfortable if every employee learned exactly how this was made and by whom?

**Separation of duties.** The Responsible Human cannot be the Second Reviewer. The Steward's own requests are reviewed by the Operations Director acting as Steward.
**No urgent lane.** There is no expedited path. Urgent communications use real people.
**Records and appeals.** Every decision, including rejections, is recorded in the register. A requester may appeal a tier classification to the Executive Sponsor, never a prohibition.

## 9. Incident response

**An incident is:** (a) Firm synthetic media published without approval or without labels; (b) an excerpt or reuse that misleads; (c) unauthorized use of any employee's or leader's likeness by anyone, inside or outside the Firm; (d) a suspected voice or video impersonation attempt on staff; (e) a revocation of consent that is not honored; (f) published content later found to be factually wrong.

| When | What |
|---|---|
| **0 to 2 hours** | Whoever notices reports to the Steward through the incident line on the intranet. The Steward alerts the Executive Sponsor, and HR if a real person is affected. |
| **Within 24 hours** | Contain: unpublish or disable, freeze related tool accounts, and **preserve evidence** (files, logs, links, screenshots; do not delete). Identify affected people and tell them in person, by a human, not with an AI-drafted message. |
| **Money or credentials involved** | Notify Finance and IT security immediately and treat as a security incident; follow the payment-recall procedure. |
| **Within 48 hours** | If the material reached beyond its intended audience or misled anyone: written all-staff correction from a named human saying what happened, what is true, and what to do. |
| **External spread** | The General Counsel decides on platform takedown requests, notices, and any regulator or law-enforcement contact. |
| **Within 10 business days** | Post-incident review; register updated; proposed policy changes go to the Executive Sponsor. |

**Reporting in good faith is protected.** Nobody is disciplined for promptly reporting their own honest mistake. Discipline is for concealment, bad faith, or violations of Section 4, which are handled under the Firm's code of conduct.

---

## 10. Refusal

Even for permitted uses, the Firm **declines** to produce synthetic media when:

- **R1.** The audience would reasonably expect a human: messages of care, apology, thanks, condolence, or recognition of individual staff.
- **R2.** The subject is contested, sensitive or time-critical: a crisis, rumors about layoffs, a client incident, health or safety, a dispute inside the Firm.
- **R3.** The source text is not final, not verified, or has unresolved caveats. Unverified claims are not delivered synthetically.
- **R4.** Consent is missing, ambiguous, recently pressured, or being reconsidered, or the person hesitates.
- **R5.** The only reason is to save a leader's time on a message they should give themselves.
- **R6.** The reviewers cannot say in one sentence who stands behind the message.
- **R7.** The tool is unapproved, or its data handling is unclear.

**Anyone can say no.** Employees may decline to appear in, or work on, synthetic media without consequence. A refusal by the Steward or a Second Reviewer stands unless the Executive Sponsor and Head of People jointly overturn a tier decision in writing; prohibitions can never be overturned.

---

## 11. Compliance, audit and sunset

- **Quarterly reconciliation.** The Steward and Operations Director compare the register against the intranet video library and shared drives to find unregistered synthetic content.
- **Monthly consent sweep** for expired or revoked consents.
- **Quarterly spot check:** two items chosen at random by the Operations Director; verify the five-question record, in-frame label placement, and hash match.
- **Confidential reporting channel** to the Head of People and the Operations Director.
- **Training:** 30 minutes at onboarding and annually, covering the callback rule and how to spot impersonation attempts.
- **What we measure:** count of unregistered items found, expired consents found, incidents, and time-to-takedown. **What we do not claim to measure:** "employee trust" or "no deception occurred." Zero reported incidents is treated as ambiguous, not as success.
- **Sunset.** This policy lapses after 12 months unless renewed following a documented review that must consider whether to narrow the permitted uses.

## 12. Worked examples

| Proposed use | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Approved synthetic presenter explains the new expense-reporting tool from the IT how-to | **Permitted (Tier A)** | Routine information from a source document; labeled; no real person. |
| The same presenter presents an analysis of whether the Firm should pilot a four-day week, ahead of a leadership decision | **Tier B** | Steward and Second Reviewer; the decision-maker confirms accuracy; caveats stay in the same segment as the claims (6.8). |
| HR wants an announcement of the annual bonus in the CEO's cloned voice | **Prohibited (P3, and P1 without consent)** | Affects pay; a leader's message about pay should come from the leader, in person or as an authentic recording (7.6). |
| A trainer wants her own recorded onboarding video dubbed into Spanish in her own voice | **Tier B1, after consent** | Written consent, human-reviewed translation, and her approval of the final. |
| Marketing wants a synthetic "employee" saying "I love working here" for a recruiting page | **Prohibited (P5, P6)** | External use and a fabricated testimonial. |
| The team wants to update twelve old training videos by cloning a departed colleague's voice | **Prohibited (P1, P7)** | Consent ended at separation. Use an approved presenter instead. |
| A voicemail in the finance director's voice asks to change a vendor's bank account | **Not a use of the policy; an incident (9d)** | Do not act; call back on a known number (7.5); report to the Steward. |
| An employee makes a "funny" clip of a manager with a personal free account | **Prohibited (P1, P9)** | No consent, unapproved tool; reportable if shared. |
| A restructuring notice delivered by the synthetic presenter for consistency | **Prohibited (P3); refused (R1, R2)** | Consequential news needs an accountable human. |
